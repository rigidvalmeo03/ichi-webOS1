Chemistry Notes

Chemistry is the study of matter, its properties, and its composition.

Quick Chemistry Facts!

  • Atoms are the basic units of matter.
  • Elements are made of one type of atom.
  • Chemical reactions form new substances.

Pure Substances!

  • Matter - Anything that has mass and occupies space.
  • Pure Substance - Has a fixed composition; includes elements and compounds.
  • Element - Made of one type of atom; cannot be broken down.
  • Compound - Made of two or more elements chemically combined.
  • Mixture - Two or more substances physically combined; can be separated.
    •

    Atoms, Molecules, and Ions

    • 1. Electronic Structure of atoms - how electrons are arranged in an atom's energy levels (or shells).
    • 2. Pauli Exclusion Principle - Each orbital can hold 2 electrons with opposite spins
    • 3. Hund's Rule - Electrons fill empty orbitals first before pairing up.
    • Photo Source: Science Notes
    • 4. Aufbau Principle - electrons fill the lowest energy first.
    • Photo Source: BYJU's The Learning App

    Chemical Bonding

    Joining of atoms to form compounds or molecules. Atoms bond to become more stable, usually by completing their outer electron shells.

    Types of Chemical Bonds:

    • 1. Ionic Bond - metals + nonmetals = ionic compounds
    • 2. Covalent Bond - nonmetals + nonmetals = molecular compounds
    • 3. Metallic Bond - metals + metals =metallic substance

    Periodicity

    Periodicity is the repeating patterns in properties of elements across the periodic table

    • 1. Atomic Radius (Size of Atom) - Across a period: more protons pull electrons closer (same shell → smaller). Down a group: more shells → atoms get bigger
    • 2. Ionization Energy (energy to remove 1 electron) - More electrons in outer shell = harder to remove. Farther from nucleus = easier to remove
    • 3. Electronegativity (pull on shared electrons) - More protons + almost full outer shell = stronger pull. Bigger atoms = weaker pull
    • 4. Metallic Character - Elements on the left (like Na, K) do this better than the right (like Cl)

    Photo Source: Mirek2 / Wikimedia Commons

    Periodic Table Groups and Families

    • Group 1: Alkali Metals - Very reactive, soft metals
    • Group 2: Alkaline Earth Metals - Reactive, found in rocks
    • Groups 3-12: Transition Metals - Hard, shiny, good conductors
    • Group 13: Boron Group - Less Reactive, Mixed types, includes aluminum
    • Group 14: Carbon Group - Forms many compounds
    • Group 15: Nitrogen Group - Used in air and fertilizers
    • Group 16: Oxygen Group - Needed for breathing, includes sulfur
    • Group 17: Halogens - Very reactive, forms salts
    • Group 18: Noble Gases - Stable, nonreactive gases
